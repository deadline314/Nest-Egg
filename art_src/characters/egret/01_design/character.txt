Egret, a veteran harbour mercenary, a woman of forty-six, clearly a woman: a woman's face, jaw and neck, a woman's lean athletic build under the work clothes; about seven heads tall, rangy with long legs. Cropped steel-grey hair shaved at the side with a longer swept top, weathered tan skin, laugh lines, a thin old scar across the cheekbone, a crooked half-grin. She wears a faded slate-grey oilskin rain jacket that ends at the belt, zipped half way with the collar turned up, the sleeves down to the wrists, one elbow patched; a dark work shirt; worn canvas cargo trousers with a knee patch, tucked into scuffed black rubber dock boots; fingerless work gloves; a folded black umbrella slung across her back on a strap; one short teal reflective strip on the jacket sleeve.
