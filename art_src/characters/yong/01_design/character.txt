Yong, the head of a harbour debt-collecting crew, a man of forty-five who used to load freight on the docks: about seven heads tall, heavy and thick through the neck, shoulders and forearms, a solid belly, standing planted. A square tired face with a broken nose, deep creases beside the mouth, heavy brows, grey-flecked stubble, short cropped black hair going grey at the temples, one ear slightly cauliflowered. He wears a faded rust-brown canvas dock jacket with the sleeves pushed up to the elbows, a dark grey undershirt, and over them a worn second-hand industrial lifting frame: dull steel struts along the back of the arm and down the spine, a scuffed hinge at the elbow, and a boxy battery case strapped on the chest behind a dented steel plate with one small amber indicator. Dark oil-stained work trousers with a wide leather belt, heavy steel-toed dock boots, strapped knee pads, bare thick hands with taped knuckles.
