The gatekeeper, a doorman of about sixty whose memory has been used up, a man: about seven heads tall, gaunt and thin-wristed, standing very still and straight like someone who has stood at the same door for decades. A long hollow-cheeked face with a mild empty gaze, thin grey hair combed neatly over, large ears, a clean-shaven chin with a small shaving nick. He wears an old-fashioned faded navy doorman's uniform: a short jacket that ends at the belt line, well above the thighs, with two rows of dull brass buttons, a stand-up collar a size too big for the neck, worn cuffs, and a plain blank white name tag on the chest; matching navy trousers with a faded side stripe; old black leather shoes polished but cracked; thin white cotton gloves gone grey.
