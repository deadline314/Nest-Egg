Vig, the deputy of a harbour debt-collecting crew, a man of about thirty-five who works the receipt counter: about seven heads tall, lean and narrow-shouldered with a slight desk-worker's slouch kept straight. A long face with a thin fixed polite smile, tired eyes with dark rings, a crooked front tooth, black hair combed flat and parted with oil, a thin moustache. He wears a cheap grey waistcoat with one mismatched button over an off-white shirt gone yellow at the collar, the sleeves rolled to the elbows and held with black sleeve garters, a narrow loosened dark red tie, a black paper-cuff protector on the forearm, dark pressed trousers shiny at the knees, scuffed black leather shoes; ink-stained fingers; a small brass stamp hanging from the belt on a short chain.
