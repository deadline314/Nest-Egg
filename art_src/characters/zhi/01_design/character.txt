Sister Zhi, the nurse who runs the front desk of a harbour clinic, a woman of thirty-six, clearly a woman: about seven heads tall, average build, brisk and upright. A plain capable face with a firm mouth, faint frown lines, dark brown hair tied in a short low ponytail with a ballpoint pen pushed through it, a few loose strands, reading glasses pushed up on the head. She wears a faded dusty-rose knitted cardigan that ends at the belt line, well above the thighs, the sleeves pushed up to the elbows, over a pale grey nurse's tunic tucked into dark navy trousers; worn white nurse's shoes with scuffed toes; a staff card on a short lanyard; a cheap wristwatch; a strip of sticking plaster on one finger.
