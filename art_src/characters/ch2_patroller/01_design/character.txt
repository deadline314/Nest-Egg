The market entrance patroller, a clearly adult man in his late forties who checks inspection passes at the harbour market gate for the market office. About seven heads tall, stocky with a slight stoop from long shifts, neutral upright posture. Short greying crew cut under a faded navy rain cap with a short stiff brim, a square tired face with a heavy jaw, a broken-and-reset nose and one ear a little larger than the other, deep lines between the brows. A dull navy hip-length rain cape-coat with a high collar, its shoulders darkened by rain and one front button replaced with a mismatched grey one; a small rectangular cloth armband in faded orange on the near upper arm as the only accent; plain charcoal work trousers tucked into scuffed black rubber boots. A slim grey clipboard hangs from his belt on a short cord at the far hip. No armour, no weapons, no badge that glows, no object held.
