# Rin hand, sleeve and rifle grip lighting audit

This private evidence set compares eight standing, grounded, two-handed rifle captures under one fixed `LightKit` environment and white key: right and left facings, with the back hand and both back sleeves at 0.62, 0.9 and 1.0, plus a control that sets all five back hand/arm/sleeve parts to 1.0. The harness keeps one `CharacterRig` and one HDR `SubViewport` alive for all captures, restores the original tint baseline before every case, and waits for three post-draw frames. `tools/viewport_capture.gd` reads HDR 2D and converts to sRGB PNG. The ten captures in `runtime-native-2620/` are the two same-facing baselines and eight cases. Their dimensions, SHA256 hashes, alpha bounds, untouched-region RGB samples, CanvasTexture/normal-map paths, per-case anchor residuals and CPU audit notes are in [`final-summary.json`](controlled-summary.json); the original engine output is in [`runtime-native-2620/manifest.json`](controlled/manifest.json).

The measured baseline loaded by that run had all five back-part tints at 0.62. In the shared worktree at the time of this evidence summary, `art/characters/rin/character.json` has these five part `tint` fields set to 1.0: `arm_back_upper`, `arm_back_lower`, `hand_back`, `jacket_sleeve_back_upper`, and `jacket_sleeve_back_lower`. The harness recorded all five as production `CanvasTexture`s with their normal maps present in every case; it did not alter diffuse or normal textures. Across the eight candidates, the saved torso, navy shoulder, and mechanical-palm interior sample RGB values exactly matched their same-facing baselines. The compared head, torso, front sleeves and mechanical-palm regions had no newly dark pixels; their maximum mean RGB difference was about 0.00123, limited to small overlaps from the tinted back parts. Front hand-to-grip residual was 0; back hand-to-foregrip residual was at most 0.000069 world pixels. These measurements support the tint comparison and anchor-coordinate coincidence only.

The earlier report that later images rendered a nearly black body was a multi-image display artifact. Raw PNG checks show `runtime-native-3177/right_hand_sleeves_0p62.png` is byte-identical to the v3 right baseline, and its torso, navy-shoulder and mechanical-palm samples remain byte-identical across all four right-facing tint cases. The same three sample points in the v2 left-facing frame are also alpha 255 and byte-identical across its four cases; its character is translated relative to v3, so compare those samples in their recorded v2 coordinates. Its raw opaque-pixel RGB distribution is normal-range. Do not describe v2 as a proven lighting or material failure.

`runtime-native-2561/` is the one rejected framing attempt: the alpha bounds touch the top edge (`y=0`) in both facings, while its grip/foregrip points were near `y=47–61` and the muzzle point was not recorded. Its head and rifle were visibly cropped, so it is not evidence for choosing tints. `runtime-native-3177/` is retained as history and its images are not rejected for black output. `runtime-native-2620/` is the controlled CPU-verified comparison with two baselines, eight passing cases and no manifest errors.

The source atlas audit remains in [`rifle-grip-audit.json`](rifle-grip-audit.json), with 1× annotation and nearest-neighbor previews beside it. `art/weapons/rifle.png` is 104×36; configured grip `(30,22)` is opaque within the handgrip silhouette, while foregrip `(64,20)` is on the underside/handguard. This confirms the coordinates point at opaque source pixels, not that the drawn fingers visibly wrap the handle. The result does not establish natural hand contact, kneeling anatomy, hip or cloth seams, or full-body acceptance. The parent is doing a separate three-light arena review; no GPU render, formal asset edit, weapon metadata change, or runtime code change was made for this CPU evidence pass.
